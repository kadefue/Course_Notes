## 1. Course Overview and Pedagogical Philosophy

**BIT 111: Microcomputers and Applications** is an introductory undergraduate foundation course designed to equip first-year university scholars with foundational digital literacy, architectural comprehension of microcomputers, practical operating system navigation, office productivity skills, and robust cybersecurity hygiene.

### Core Pedagogical Principles
1. **Introductory & Conceptual Design:** The curriculum is calibrated specifically for first-year undergraduate students entering university from diverse academic backgrounds. It assumes no prior programming or computer science experience.
2. **Zero-Calculation Architecture Rule:** In strict adherence to university introductory guidelines, **Topics 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6 contain strictly NO mathematical formulas or engineering calculations** (no clock cycle equations, CPI, MIPS, Amdahl's Law, password entropy logarithms, or electrical power factor calculations). 
3. **Simple Spreadsheet Mathematics (Topic 5 Exemption):** Basic mathematical and statistical modeling is focused exclusively within **Topic 5 (Spreadsheet Applications)**, where students master fundamental arithmetic operators (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`) and essential summary functions (`SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `COUNT`, `COUNTA`, `MIN`, `MAX`, and basic single-condition `IF`).
4. **Inclusive Cross-Disciplinary Contextualization:** Every lecture, compendium chapter, case study, and assignment question is anchored directly within real-world agricultural, natural science, and socioeconomic domains across Sokoine University of Agriculture:
   - **Agronomy, Horticulture & Crop Science**
   - **Animal Science & Livestock Production**
   - **Veterinary Medicine & Biomedical Sciences**
   - **Wildlife Management, Ecotourism & Hospitality**
   - **Agricultural Engineering, Irrigation & Water Resources**
   - **Agricultural Extension & Community Development**
   - **Aquaculture & Aquatic Sciences**
   - **Forestry, Wood Science & Technology**
   - **Food Science, Technology & Agro-Processing**
   - **Family, Nutrition & Consumer Studies**

## 2. Course Expected Learning Outcomes (CELOs)

Upon successful completion of BIT 111, first-year scholars will demonstrate competence across Bloom's Revised Taxonomy:

- **CELO 1 (Remember & Understand):** Define and articulate fundamental computing concepts, the Information Processing Cycle (IPOS+C), the Von Neumann architectural model, and the chronological evolution across the five computer generations.
- **CELO 2 (Hardware Anatomy):** Identify, locate, and explain the physical components of the microcomputer system unit (CPU, motherboard, cache hierarchy, volatile RAM, non-volatile ROM/firmware, secondary storage media, and peripheral I/O interfaces).
- **CELO 3 (Security Controls):** Implement multi-tiered personal computer security controls, including physical chassis locks, power-on/HDD passwords, BIOS/UEFI setup passwords, and operating system authentication.
- **CELO 4 (Operating Systems):** Navigate, configure, and manage system resources using command-line interfaces (MS-DOS internal and external commands) and Graphical User Interfaces (Windows management consoles and Linux shell fundamentals).
- **CELO 5 (Application Productivity):** Produce professional academic manuscripts, format complex tables, execute automated mail merges, construct dynamic spreadsheets with simple formulas and summary functions, and design professional presentation graphics.
- **CELO 6 (Data Defense & Hygiene):** Apply information security principles (the CIA Triad), identify malware categories (viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, phishing), maintain antivirus definitions, and follow strict USB flash drive hygiene in campus computer laboratories.

## 4. Course Curriculum & Modular Syllabus

The syllabus is structured into **six comprehensive pedagogical topics**:

### Topic 1: Introduction to Microcomputers
- **1.1 Understanding Computers & the Digital Era:** Data, Information, Knowledge, and Wisdom (DIKW hierarchy); the Information Processing Cycle (Input, Processing, Output, Storage, Communication); Sokoine University of Agriculture digital transformation agenda.
- **1.2 The Von Neumann Architectural Model:** Stored-program concept; CPU subsystems (Control Unit, ALU, Registers); System Buses (Address, Data, Control); Fetch-Decode-Execute machine cycle; Von Neumann bottleneck and modern mitigations.
- **1.3 Computer Generations:** 1st Gen (Vacuum tubes, ENIAC/UNIVAC); 2nd Gen (Transistors, FORTRAN/COBOL); 3rd Gen (Integrated circuits, Minicomputers, OS timesharing); 4th Gen (VLSI, Microprocessors, Personal Computers); 5th Gen (Artificial Intelligence, Parallel processing, Quantum computing principles).
- **1.4 Pioneers of Computing:** Charles Babbage, Ada Lovelace, Alan Turing, John von Neumann, Grace Murray Hopper, Robert Noyce, Jack Kilby, Ted Hoff, Federico Faggin, Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, Bill Gates, Paul Allen, Tim Berners-Lee.
- **1.5 Classification of Computers:** Microcomputers (Desktops, Laptops, Tablets, Smartphones, SBCs); Technical Workstations; Minicomputers / Departmental Servers; Mainframe Computers; Supercomputers; Embedded Systems.

### Topic 2: Main Components of a Microcomputer
- **2.1 The System Unit & Motherboard Architecture:** Motherboard layout, form factors (ATX, microATX, Mini-ITX); CPU socket, RAM DIMM slots, PCIe expansion slots, chipset (Northbridge/Southbridge / PCH), power connectors.
- **2.2 The Central Processing Unit (CPU):** Internal architecture (CU, ALU, Registers: PC, IR, MAR, MBR, AC, Flags); System Clock concept (GHz as frequency pulses, non-mathematical); Multi-core processors (dual, quad, octa-core); Cache memory hierarchy (L1, L2, L3).
- **2.3 Primary Memory Subsystems:** Volatile RAM (DRAM vs. SRAM, DDR evolution through DDR5); Non-volatile ROM (PROM, EPROM, EEPROM, Flash memory for BIOS/UEFI); CMOS RAM and Real-Time Clock (RTC) backed by CR2032 battery.
- **2.4 Secondary Storage Media:** Magnetic storage (HDD platters, tracks, sectors, cylinders, read/write heads); Solid-state storage (NAND flash, SSDs, SATA vs. NVMe M.2); Optical storage (CD, DVD, Blu-ray pits and lands); Removable flash drives; Cloud repositories.
- **2.5 Input & Output Devices:** Keyboards (QWERTY, membrane vs. mechanical), pointing devices (optical mouse, trackpad, touchscreen), optical scanners, barcode/QR readers, RFID livestock transponders, biometric sensors; Display monitors (LCD, LED, OLED, resolution, refresh rate), printers (Impact dot-matrix vs. Non-impact inkjet, laser, wide-format plotters), audio peripherals, physical actuators.
- **2.6 Comparative Hardware Aspects:** Microcomputer vs. Mainframe; Technical Workstation vs. Standard PC; Minicomputer vs. Microcomputer; Desktop vs. Laptop internal architecture (cooling, thermal throttling, upgradeability).

### Topic 3: PC Security Controls
- **3.1 Physical Chassis & Hardware Security:** Physical chassis intrusion switches, Kensington security lock slots, tamper-evident security seals, locked server enclosures, securing field telemetry stations.
- **3.2 Hardware-Level Password Controls:** Power-On Passwords (authenticating before POST); Hard Disk Passwords (ATA drive locking); BIOS/UEFI Setup Passwords (Supervisor vs. User privilege levels); CMOS battery jumper reset mechanics.
- **3.3 Operating System User Authentication:** User account architectures (Administrator vs. Standard user); Principle of Least Privilege; Graphical login passwords, PINs, and Windows Hello biometric authentication; Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
- **3.4 Institutional Research Protection at SUA:** Safeguarding confidential veterinary clinical patient files, undergraduate examination grades on SUASIS, multi-year agronomic trial databases, and endangered wildlife GPS tracking coordinates.

### Topic 4: Functions & Types of Operating Systems
- **4.1 Core Operating System Functions:** Hardware abstraction layer; Processor management (process scheduling concepts, multitasking); Memory management (virtual memory, paging concepts without math); Storage & File management; Device driver coordination; User interface provision.
- **4.2 Classifications of Operating Systems:** Single-user vs. Multi-user; Single-tasking vs. Multitasking; Batch, Time-sharing, Real-time (RTOS), and Network operating systems (NOS).
- **4.3 Command-Line Navigation (MS-DOS):** Architecture of command interpreters; Internal commands (`DIR`, `CD`, `MD`, `RD`, `COPY`, `DEL`, `REN`, `TYPE`, `CLS`, `VER`, `DATE`, `TIME`) vs. External commands (`FORMAT`, `CHKDSK`, `XCOPY`, `DISKCOPY`, `ATTRIB`, `TREE`); Path syntax (absolute vs. relative paths).
- **4.4 Graphical Operating Systems (Windows & Linux):** Windows administrative utilities (File Explorer, Task Manager performance monitoring, Device Manager, Disk Management, Control Panel); Linux open-source architecture (monolithic kernel, hierarchical filesystem `/`, terminal commands: `ls`, `cd`, `mkdir`, `cp`, `mv`, `rm`, `cat`, `df -h`, `free -h`, `uname -a`).

### Topic 5: Microcomputer Applications
- **5.1 Word Processing Systems:** User interface, margin geometry, typography, paragraph styles, automated tables of contents, academic citations, referencing, and automated Mail Merge workflows for community extension bulletins.
- **5.2 Spreadsheet Modeling & Simple Calculations:** Spreadsheet anatomy (workbooks, sheets, columns, rows, cells); Cell referencing (Relative `A1` vs. Absolute `$A$1`); Data formatting (currency, percentages, dates); **Simple Arithmetic Calculations** (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, order of operations); **Essential Functions** (`SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `COUNT`, `COUNTA`, `MIN`, `MAX`, basic logical `IF`); Generating clear bar, column, line, and pie charts for agricultural and clinical datasets.
- **5.3 Presentation Graphics:** Slide design principles (6x6 rule, visual contrast, minimal cognitive load); Integrating diagrams, tables, and multimedia; Professional academic delivery across university lecture halls.
- **5.4 Internet, Web, and Collaborative Applications:** Web browsers, search engines, URLs, domain names; Email protocols (SMTP, POP3, IMAP); Professional academic email netiquette; Collaborative cloud productivity suites (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365) in interdisciplinary research.

### Topic 6: Data Security and Malware Defense
- **6.1 Environmental & Physical Hazards:** Ambient dust, tropical humidity, excessive heat, liquid spills; Electrical power anomalies (blackouts, brownouts, sags, surges, electrical noise); Surge suppressors, voltage stabilizers, Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS); The 3-2-1 Backup Strategy.
- **6.2 Information Security & Access Controls:** The CIA Triad (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability); Strong password construction rules (length, complexity, non-dictionary phrases); Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA); Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
- **6.3 Comprehensive Malware Taxonomy:** Computer Viruses (file-infector, macro, boot-sector); Network Worms (autonomous self-propagation); Trojan Horses (disguised payloads); Ransomware (cryptographic extortion); Spyware, Keyloggers, Adware, and Rootkits; Social Engineering, Phishing, and Smishing tactics.
- **6.4 Antivirus & Endpoint Defense Strategies:** Signature-based scanning; Heuristic behavioral analysis; Real-time background shields; Quarantining infected files; Maintaining daily definition updates; Laboratory USB flash drive hygiene protocols; Disaster recovery and system restoration.

## 8. Departmental Laboratory Guidelines & Safety Code

Practical laboratory exercises are hosted in the **DIIT Undergraduate Computer Laboratories** (Main Campus and Solomon Mahlangu Campus). All students must adhere to the following professional code of conduct:

1. **Food and Liquid Ban:** Strictly no beverages (including bottled water) or foodstuffs are allowed near computer desks. Liquid spills cause catastrophic short-circuits, motherboard damage, and electrical shock hazards.
2. **Electrical Safety:** Do not tamper with power cords, unseat surge protectors, or pull cables abruptly. Never attempt manual electrical repairs; notify the laboratory systems administrator immediately.
3. **Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) Protection:** When opening computer cases during hardware maintenance sessions, always wear an anti-static wrist strap clipped to the metal chassis to prevent frying microchips.
4. **Removable Media Quarantine:** All personal USB flash drives must be scanned with university antivirus software upon insertion before opening files to protect campus local networks from malware.
5. **System Settings Integrity:** Do not alter desktop display resolutions, disconnect keyboards/mice, delete system files, or install unauthorized third-party software.
6. **Ergonomic Best Practices:** Adjust chairs so feet rest flat with knees at 90 degrees; position monitors at arm's length with screen tops at eye level; keep wrists level; and adhere to the **20-20-20 Rule** (every 20 minutes, focus on an object 20 feet away for 20 seconds).
